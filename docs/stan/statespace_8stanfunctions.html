Equation (3) in Vanko et al (2026), Section 2.2.1, specifies the demographic transition as a multinomial over survival, hunting and other deaths

\[ \mathbf{u}_{a,s,t}^{1}\sim\mathrm{Multinomial}\left( n_{a,s,t},\left(\rho^{S},\rho^{H^{\mathrm{sw}}}, \rho^{H^{\mathrm{fi}}},\rho^{D}\right)_{a,s,t}\right) \]

Section 2.4 describes a continuous log-Gaussian approximation for fitting This function uses a logistic-normal approximation rather than an exact multinomial draw For the active categories, it constructs log-ratio means and a covariance factor from adjusted expected counts and returns

\[ \mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{active}}=N\operatorname{softmax} \left(\begin{bmatrix}0\\\mathbf{m}+L\mathbf{z}\end{bmatrix}\right) \]

This last expression documents the implementation, not a numbered paper equation It gives continuous non-negative allocations summing to the supplied population It does not imply exact multinomial moments

Mapping from paper notation to code

  • \( n_{a,s,t} \): demographic_population_count
  • \( n_{a,s,t}\rho^I_{a,s,t} \): the corresponding entry of expected_fate_counts
  • \( \mathbf{z} \): the required entries of fate_allocation_noise
  • \( \mathbf{m},L \): log-ratio mean and covariance factor computed below

Category order matches Equation (3): survival, Swedish hunting, Finnish hunting, other death or bycatch Countries with zero quotas are omitted from the active categories and their allocations remain exactly zero, using one fewer innovation than active fates

Equation (78) in Vanko et al (2026), Appendix D.2.1, specifies

\[ \exp(-\theta_0) \sim \mathrm{Uniform}\left(\max_t b_0^t, 1\right) \]

The code parameterizes the survival of pregnancies at zero seal density using the attainable upper baseline birth rate and a scaled value

\[ q_0 = b_{\max}^0 + d\left(1-b_{\max}^0\right), \qquad \theta_0 = -\log(q_0) \]

This transformation is the implementation's parameterization, not a separately numbered equation in the paper The code uses \( b_{\max}^0 \) rather than the maximum over yearly baseline birth rates appearing in Equation (78)

Mapping from mathematical notation to code

  • \( b_{\max}^0 \): birth_rate_baseline_max
  • \( d \): density_dependence_scaled
  • \( \theta_0 \): the returned density-dependence intercept

The two logarithm branches evaluate the same expression while avoiding loss of precision near zero or one

Equation (51) in Vanko et al (2026), Appendix C.1, parameterizes the slope at reference herring conditions

\[ \theta_1 = \frac{1}{\bar K} \log\left[1-\frac{1}{\theta_0} \log\left(\frac{b_K}{\bar b_0}\right)\right] \]

This follows by solving Equation (10) for the slope at population size \( \bar K \) and baseline birth rate \( \bar b_0 \)

Mapping from paper notation to code

  • \( b_K/\bar b_0 \): birth_rate_at_capacity_to_baseline_ratio
  • \( \theta_0 \): density_dependence_intercept
  • \( \bar K \): carrying_capacity

The implementation takes the logarithm of the supplied ratio internally and uses log1p_exp to avoid constructing a potentially overflowing quotient The non-degenerate density-suppression case requires positive intercept and carrying capacity and \( 0 < b_K/\bar b_0 < 1 \)

Combines Equations (64), (71), and (72) in Vanko et al (2026), Appendix D with the log selectivity in Equation (73)

\[ E_{i,t}^{C}=\Psi_i^{C}\exp(-\epsilon_t^{C})\widehat E_t^{C},\qquad \widehat E_t^{C}=\frac{2Q_t^{C}}{k^2\sum_j n_{j,t}\Psi_j^{C}},\qquad \Psi_i^{C}=\exp(g_i^{C}) \]

The equivalent expression evaluated by the code is

\[ E_{i,t}^{C}=\frac{2Q_t^{C}\exp(g_i^{C}-\sigma_C z_t^{C})} {k^2\sum_j n_{j,t}\exp(g_j^{C})} \]

Mapping from paper notation to code

  • \( Q_t^{C} \): hunting_quota
  • \( g_i^{C} \): hunting_selectivity[i], on the log scale
  • \( n_{i,t} \): population_comp[i]
  • \( k \): t_birth_to_end_hunt
  • \( \sigma_C \): hunting_effort_sd
  • \( z_t^{C} \): hunting_effort_noise, a standardized non-negative innovation
  • \( \epsilon_t^{C}=\sigma_C z_t^{C} \): the paper's effort reduction

The factor \( 2/k^2 \) follows from integrating the linearly declining hazard \( E_{i,t}^{C}(k-\tau) \) over the hunting interval The constant-population approximation calibrates the quota-based pressure The subsequent ODE calculation accounts for depletion during hunting A zero quota returns zero pressure and positive quotas use log-space arithmetic

Equation (48) in Vanko et al (2026), Appendix C.1, follows from the Euler-Lotka equation (47) at population growth rate \( \lambda = 1 \)

\[ b_K = 2\frac{1-\phi_{f,5+}}{\prod_{i=0}^{4}\phi_{f,i}} \]

The factor of two accounts for the equal female and male pup sex ratio This is the replacement birth rate in the absence of hunting Since \( \phi_{f,i}=\exp(-\mu_{f,i}) \), the equivalent expression is

\[ b_K = 2(1-\phi_{f,5+})\exp\left(\sum_{i=0}^{4}\mu_{f,i}\right) \]

Mapping from paper notation to code

  • \( \phi_{f,5+} \): female_adult_survival_probability
  • \( \mu_{f,0},\ldots,\mu_{f,4} \): mu_m[1:(n_age_classes - 1)]
  • The paper has six age classes per sex, with the last containing ages five and older

The implementation generalizes the sum to the supplied number of age classes and computes the replacement rate on the log scale internally Set log_scale to one to return that log value directly for likelihood calculations A mathematical replacement rate above one indicates that replacement cannot be achieved with at most one pup per adult female per year

Parameters

n_age_classesNumber of age classes per sex including the adult class
female_adult_survival_probabilityAnnual non-hunting survival probability of adult females
log_scaleReturn the log replacement rate if one or the replacement rate if zero
Returns
Birth rate required for demographic replacement without hunting, on the requested scale

Equation (4) in Vanko et al (2026), Section 2.2.1, interpolates non-hunting mortality between pups and adults for each sex

\[ \log(\mu_{a,s}) = \log(\mu_{0,s}) + \left(\frac{a}{5}\right)^c \left[\log(\mu_{5+,s})-\log(\mu_{0,s})\right],\qquad 1\le a\le4 \]

Female endpoint mortality rates are obtained from annual survival probabilities

\[ \mu_{0,f}=-\log(\phi_{0,f}),\qquad \mu_{5+,f}=-\log(\phi_{5+,f}) \]

Equations (60) and (61) in Appendix D.2 express male mortality as deviations from female mortality on the log scale

\[ \log(\mu_{0,m})=\log(\mu_{0,f})+\nu_0,\qquad \log(\mu_{5+,m})=\log(\mu_{5+,f})+\nu_{5+} \]

Mapping from paper notation to code

  • \( \phi_{0,f} \): female_pup_survival_probability
  • \( \phi_{5+,f} \): female_adult_survival_probability
  • \( c \): mortality_age_shape
  • \( \nu_0 \): male_pup_offset
  • \( \nu_{5+} \): male_adult_offset

Female age classes precede male age classes in the returned vector With six age classes, code index j corresponds to age \( a=j-1 \) The code generalizes the denominator five to n_age_classes - 1 These rates include natural mortality and bycatch but exclude hunting
