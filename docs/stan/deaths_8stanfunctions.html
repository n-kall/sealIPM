Equation (4) in Vanko et al (2026), Section 2.2.1, interpolates non-hunting mortality between pups and adults for each sex

\[ \log(\mu_{a,s}) = \log(\mu_{0,s}) + \left(\frac{a}{5}\right)^c \left[\log(\mu_{5+,s})-\log(\mu_{0,s})\right],\qquad 1\le a\le4 \]

Female endpoint mortality rates are obtained from annual survival probabilities

\[ \mu_{0,f}=-\log(\phi_{0,f}),\qquad \mu_{5+,f}=-\log(\phi_{5+,f}) \]

Equations (60) and (61) in Appendix D.2 express male mortality as deviations from female mortality on the log scale

\[ \log(\mu_{0,m})=\log(\mu_{0,f})+\nu_0,\qquad \log(\mu_{5+,m})=\log(\mu_{5+,f})+\nu_{5+} \]

Mapping from paper notation to code

  • \( \phi_{0,f} \): female_pup_survival_probability
  • \( \phi_{5+,f} \): female_adult_survival_probability
  • \( c \): mortality_age_shape
  • \( \nu_0 \): male_pup_mortality_log_offset
  • \( \nu_{5+} \): male_adult_mortality_log_offset

Female age classes precede male age classes in the returned vector

With six age classes, age is one less than the code index age_index

The code generalizes the denominator five to n_age_classes - 1

These rates include natural mortality and bycatch but exclude hunting

A positive male log mortality offset multiplies mortality by exp(offset) and therefore reduces survival, rather than increasing it
