Equation (11) in Vanko et al (2026), Section 2.2.2

\[ b_0^t(\mathbf{h}_{t-1}) = b_{\min}^0 + \frac{b_{\max}^0 - b_{\min}^0} {1 + \exp\left[-\left(\alpha + \beta \left(w h_{t-1}^{\mathrm{BP+GoF}} + (1-w) h_{t-1}^{\mathrm{GoB}}\right)\right)\right]} \]

This is the intrinsic birth rate before population density effects

The herring indices describe normalized weight-at-age in the Baltic Proper and Gulf of Finland (BP+GoF) and the Gulf of Bothnia (GoB)

Mapping from paper notation to code

  • \( b_{\min}^0 \): birth_rate_baseline_min
  • \( b_{\max}^0 \): birth_rate_baseline_max
  • \( \beta \): herring_slope
  • \( \alpha \): -herring_slope * herring_birth_rate_midpoint
  • \( w \): herring_weight

The code uses the equivalent logistic predictor herring_slope * (weighted_h - herring_birth_rate_midpoint)

The midpoint is -alpha / beta in weighted herring-index units

At the midpoint the baseline birth rate is halfway between its limits

If the slope is zero the curve is constant and the midpoint is not identified

Equation (48) in Vanko et al (2026), Appendix C.1, follows from the Euler-Lotka equation (47) at population growth rate \( \lambda = 1 \)

\[ b_K = 2\frac{1-\phi_{f,5+}}{\prod_{i=0}^{4}\phi_{f,i}} \]

The factor of two accounts for the equal female and male pup sex ratio

This is the replacement birth rate in the absence of hunting

Since \( \phi_{f,i}=\exp(-\mu_{f,i}) \), the equivalent expression is

\[ b_K = 2(1-\phi_{f,5+})\exp\left(\sum_{i=0}^{4}\mu_{f,i}\right) \]

Mapping from paper notation to code

  • \( \phi_{f,5+} \): female_adult_survival_probability
  • \( \mu_{f,0},\ldots,\mu_{f,4} \): non_hunting_mortality_rate[1:(n_age_classes - 1)]
  • The paper has six age classes per sex, with the last containing ages five and older

The implementation generalizes the sum to the supplied number of age classes and computes the replacement rate on the log scale internally

Set log_scale to one to return that log value directly for likelihood calculations

A mathematical replacement rate above one indicates that replacement cannot be achieved with at most one pup per adult female per year

Parameters

n_age_classesNumber of age classes per sex including the adult class
female_adult_survival_probabilityAnnual non-hunting survival probability of adult females
log_scaleReturn the log replacement rate if one or the replacement rate if zero
Returns
Birth rate required for demographic replacement without hunting, on the requested scale

real compute_density_dependence_intercept ( real  birth_rate_baseline_max,

Equation (78) in Vanko et al (2026), Appendix D.2.1, specifies

\[ \exp(-\theta_0) \sim \mathrm{Uniform}\left(\max_t b_0^t, 1\right) \]

The code parameterizes the survival of pregnancies at zero seal density using the attainable upper baseline birth rate and a scaled value

\[ q_0 = b_{\max}^0 + d\left(1-b_{\max}^0\right), \qquad \theta_0 = -\log(q_0) \]

This transformation is the implementation's parameterization, not a separately numbered equation in the paper

The code uses \( b_{\max}^0 \) rather than the maximum over yearly baseline birth rates appearing in Equation (78)

Mapping from mathematical notation to code

  • \( b_{\max}^0 \): birth_rate_baseline_max
  • \( d \): density_dependence_scaled
  • \( \theta_0 \): the returned density-dependence intercept

The two logarithm branches evaluate the same expression while avoiding loss of precision near zero or one

birth_rate_baseline_maxMaximum baseline birth rate

Equation (51) in Vanko et al (2026), Appendix C.1, parameterizes the slope at reference herring conditions

\[ \theta_1 = \frac{1}{\bar K} \log\left[1-\frac{1}{\theta_0} \log\left(\frac{b_K}{\bar b_0}\right)\right] \]

This follows by solving Equation (10) for the slope at population size \( \bar K \) and baseline birth rate \( \bar b_0 \)

Mapping from paper notation to code

  • \( b_K/\bar b_0 \): birth_rate_at_capacity_to_baseline_ratio
  • \( \theta_0 \): density_dependence_intercept
  • \( \bar K \): carrying_capacity

The implementation takes the logarithm of the supplied ratio internally and uses log1p_exp to avoid constructing a potentially overflowing quotient

The non-degenerate density-suppression case requires positive intercept and carrying capacity and \( 0 < b_K/\bar b_0 < 1 \)

Equation (53) in Vanko et al (2026), Appendix C.2

\[ p_t(\tau_s) = b_0^{t+1} \exp\left[-\theta_0\left( \frac{16}{9}\tau_s^2\exp(\theta_1 n_t)-1\right)\right] \]

In the paper \( \tau_s \) is elapsed time in years after implantation

The code argument pregnancy_exposure_scaled receives the already scaled exposure

\[ s = \frac{16}{9}\tau_s^2,\qquad p_t = b_0^{t+1}\exp\left[\theta_0-\theta_0 s\exp(\theta_1 n_t)\right] \]

The default pregnancy_exposure_scaled = 1/36 equals \( (16/9)(1.5/12)^2 \), corresponding to sampling 1.5 months after implantation

Do not pass an unscaled time in years directly to this function

Mapping from paper notation to code

  • \( b_0^{t+1} \): birth_rate_baseline, supplied from the next year's baseline
  • \( \theta_0 \): density_dependence_intercept
  • \( \theta_1 \): density_dependence_slope
  • \( n_t \): population_total
  • \( (16/9)\tau_s^2 \): pregnancy_exposure_scaled
