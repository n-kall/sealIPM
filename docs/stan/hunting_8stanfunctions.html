Equations (32), (34), and (35) in Vanko et al (2026), Appendix B, supply the four probabilities used in the multinomial transition of Equation (3)

Annual survival is evaluated analytically

\[ \rho_{i,t}^{S}=\exp\left[-\frac{k^2}{2} (E_{i,t}^{\mathrm{sw}}+E_{i,t}^{\mathrm{fi}})-\mu_i\right] \]

The paper obtains each hunting probability from its integrated hunting count

\[ \rho_{i,t}^{H^C}=\frac{H_{i,t}^{C}(k)}{n_{i,t}}, \qquad C\in\{\mathrm{sw},\mathrm{fi}\} \]

The remaining probability combines natural mortality and bycatch

\[ \rho_{i,t}^{D}=1-\rho_{i,t}^{S} -\rho_{i,t}^{H^{\mathrm{sw}}}-\rho_{i,t}^{H^{\mathrm{fi}}} \]

This implementation integrates hunting_count_derivative twice from zero to k with initial hunting count zero and population_at_hunting_start equal to one

Sweden is the focal country in the first solve and Finland in the second

The resulting counts per unit hunting-onset population are multiplied by survival from birth to hunting onset to obtain birth-referenced probabilities

A zero focal pressure or zero hunting duration skips that solve and returns zero hunting

Timing convention relative to Appendix B
Equation (26) distinguishes population just after birth from population at hunting onset

\[ \widetilde n_{i,t}^{0}=n_{i,t}\exp(-\mu_i\tau_0) \]

Substituting this into Equation (34) gives the birth-referenced probability

\[ \rho_{i,t}^{H^C}=\exp(-\mu_i\tau_0) \int_0^k E_{i,t}^{C}(k-\tau) \exp\left[-(E_{i,t}^{\mathrm{sw}}+E_{i,t}^{\mathrm{fi}}) \left(k\tau-\frac{\tau^2}{2}\right)-\mu_i\tau\right]\,d\tau \]

The pre-hunting factor is applied after each unit-population solve

Annual survival already includes the full annual mortality hazard and is not multiplied by this factor again

Mapping from paper notation to code

  • \( \tau_0 \): t_birth_to_start_hunt, time from birth to hunting onset
  • \( k \): hunting_duration, shared season duration in years
  • \( E_{i,t}^{\mathrm{sw}} \): hunting_pressure_sweden
  • \( E_{i,t}^{\mathrm{fi}} \): hunting_pressure_finland
  • \( \mu_i \): non_hunting_mortality_rate, a mortality hazard rather than a death probability

The RK45 implementation uses ode_rk45_tol with explicit tolerances and a step limit

These settings and the final probability correction are numerical controls, not additional parameters of the paper's biological model

Only probability violations within probability_tolerance are corrected

The continuous process runner computes conditional expected counts as \( \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{u}_{i,t}^{1}\mid n_{i,t},\boldsymbol{\rho}_{i,t}] =n_{i,t}\boldsymbol{\rho}_{i,t} \), derived from Equation (3) It then passes these counts to allocate_fates

The discrete runner instead uses the probabilities in multinomial_rng

Numerical probability safeguards
Equation (3) in Vanko et al (2026) requires a probability simplex

\[ \rho^I\geq0,\qquad \rho^S+\rho^{H^{\mathrm{sw}}}+\rho^{H^{\mathrm{fi}}}+\rho^D=1 \]

Equation (35) defines other death as the remainder after survival and hunting

Numerical ODE solutions can leave small negative remainders or small deviations from a unit sum due to solver and floating-point error

This correction is an implementation safeguard, not a numbered equation or additional biological assumption in the paper

After checking the original probabilities and their sum against the tolerance, it applies

\[ \widehat\rho^I= \frac{\max(0,\rho^I)}{\sum_J\max(0,\rho^J)} \]

Materially negative probabilities and material conservation errors are rejected

The tolerance is not a statistical variance parameter and does not make materially invalid probabilities acceptable

Combines Equations (64), (71), and (72) in Vanko et al (2026), Appendix D with the log selectivity in Equation (73)

\[ E_{i,t}^{C}=\Psi_i^{C}\exp(-\epsilon_t^{C})\widehat E_t^{C},\qquad \widehat E_t^{C}=\frac{2Q_t^{C}}{k^2\sum_j n_{j,t}\Psi_j^{C}},\qquad \Psi_i^{C}=\exp(g_i^{C}) \]

The equivalent expression evaluated by the code is

\[ E_{i,t}^{C}=\frac{2Q_t^{C}\exp(g_i^{C}-\sigma_C z_t^{C})} {k^2\sum_j n_{j,t}\exp(g_j^{C})} \]

Mapping from paper notation to code

  • \( Q_t^{C} \): hunting_quota
  • \( g_i^{C} \): hunting_selectivity[i], on the log scale
  • \( n_{i,t} \): population_comp[i]
  • \( k \): hunting_duration
  • \( \sigma_C \): hunting_effort_sd
  • \( z_t^{C} \): hunting_effort_noise, a standardized non-negative innovation
  • \( \epsilon_t^{C}=\sigma_C z_t^{C} \): the paper's effort reduction

The factor \( 2/k^2 \) follows from integrating the linearly declining hazard \( E_{i,t}^{C}(k-\tau) \) over the hunting interval

The constant-population approximation calibrates the quota-based pressure

The subsequent ODE calculation accounts for depletion during hunting

A zero quota returns zero pressure and positive quotas use log-space arithmetic

The same function is used for continuous fitting and discrete forecasting

Empty demographic groups are allowed and receive exactly zero pressure

A positive quota requires a positive total population

Equations (5) and (6) in Vanko et al (2026), Section 2.2.1, and Equations (18), (19), and (33) in Appendix B specify

\[ \frac{dH_{i,t}^{C}}{d\tau} =\widetilde n_{i,t}(\tau)E_{i,t}^{C}(k-\tau), \qquad 0\leq\tau\leq k \]

Substituting the surviving population from Equation (22) gives

\[ \widetilde n_{i,t}(\tau)=\widetilde n_{i,t}^{0} \exp\left[-(E_{i,t}^{\mathrm{sw}}+E_{i,t}^{\mathrm{fi}}) \left(k\tau-\frac{\tau^2}{2}\right)-\mu_i\tau\right] \]

The focal hunting count therefore depends on population depletion by both countries' hunting and non-hunting mortality

Mapping from paper notation to code

  • \( \tau \): time_since_hunting_start, elapsed time since hunting starts
  • \( H_{i,t}^{C} \): cumulative_hunting_count[1], cumulative expected focal-country hunting
  • \( \widetilde n_{i,t}^{0} \): population_at_hunting_start, population at the start of the integration
  • \( k \): hunting_duration, hunting duration and integration endpoint
  • \( E_{i,t}^{C} \): hunting_pressure_focal, focal-country hunting pressure
  • The other country's hunting pressure: hunting_pressure_competing
  • \( \mu_i \): non_hunting_mortality_rate, annual non-hunting mortality hazard

The derivative does not depend on cumulative_hunting_count because the population is available analytically rather than included as another ODE state

The caller integrates only from zero to k, outside which the paper sets the hunting derivative to zero

The vector state and separate arguments implement Stan's current solver interface without changing the differential equation
