Equations (32), (34), and (35) in Vanko et al (2026), Appendix B, supply the four probabilities used in the multinomial transition of Equation (3) Annual survival is evaluated analytically

\[ \rho_{i,t}^{S}=\exp\left[-\frac{k^2}{2} (E_{i,t}^{\mathrm{sw}}+E_{i,t}^{\mathrm{fi}})-\mu_i\right] \]

The paper obtains each hunting probability from its integrated hunting count

\[ \rho_{i,t}^{H^C}=\frac{H_{i,t}^{C}(k)}{n_{i,t}}, \qquad C\in\{\mathrm{sw},\mathrm{fi}\} \]

The remaining probability combines natural mortality and bycatch

\[ \rho_{i,t}^{D}=1-\rho_{i,t}^{S} -\rho_{i,t}^{H^{\mathrm{sw}}}-\rho_{i,t}^{H^{\mathrm{fi}}} \]

This implementation integrates hunting_count_derivative twice from zero to k with initial hunting count zero and n0 equal to one Sweden is the focal country in the first solve and Finland in the second The resulting counts per unit initial population are used as hunting probabilities A zero focal pressure or zero hunting duration skips that solve and returns zero hunting

Timing convention relative to Appendix B Equation (26) distinguishes population just after birth from population at hunting onset

\[ \widetilde n_{i,t}^{0}=n_{i,t}\exp(-\mu_i\tau_0) \]

Substituting this into Equation (34) gives the birth-referenced probability

\[ \rho_{i,t}^{H^C}=\exp(-\mu_i\tau_0) \int_0^k E_{i,t}^{C}(k-\tau) \exp\left[-(E_{i,t}^{\mathrm{sw}}+E_{i,t}^{\mathrm{fi}}) \left(k\tau-\frac{\tau^2}{2}\right)-\mu_i\tau\right]\,d\tau \]

This function has no tau0 argument and does not apply the pre-hunting factor Its unit-population solve retains the restored original implementation's hunting-onset convention, while survival uses the full annual mortality hazard This distinction is documented rather than changed here

Mapping from paper notation to code

  • \( k \): t_birth_to_end_hunt, used as hunting duration despite its name
  • \( E_{i,t}^{\mathrm{sw}} \): hunting_pressure_sweden
  • \( E_{i,t}^{\mathrm{fi}} \): hunting_pressure_finland
  • \( \mu_i \): mu_m, a mortality hazard rather than a death probability

The RK45 implementation uses ode_rk45_tol with explicit tolerances and a step limit These settings and the final probability correction are numerical controls, not additional parameters of the paper's biological model Only probability violations within probability_tolerance are corrected The continuous process runner computes conditional expected counts as \( \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{u}_{i,t}^{1}\mid n_{i,t},\boldsymbol{\rho}_{i,t}] =n_{i,t}\boldsymbol{\rho}_{i,t} \), derived from Equation (3) It then passes these counts to allocate_fates The discrete runner instead uses the probabilities in multinomial_rng

Numerical probability safeguards Equation (3) in Vanko et al (2026) requires a probability simplex

\[ \rho^I\geq0,\qquad \rho^S+\rho^{H^{\mathrm{sw}}}+\rho^{H^{\mathrm{fi}}}+\rho^D=1 \]

Equation (35) defines other death as the remainder after survival and hunting Numerical ODE solutions can leave small negative remainders or small deviations from a unit sum due to solver and floating-point error

This correction is an implementation safeguard, not a numbered equation or additional biological assumption in the paper After checking the original probabilities and their sum against the tolerance, it applies

\[ \widehat\rho^I= \frac{\max(0,\rho^I)}{\sum_J\max(0,\rho^J)} \]

Materially negative probabilities and material conservation errors are rejected The tolerance is not a statistical variance parameter and does not make materially invalid probabilities acceptable

Equations (5) and (6) in Vanko et al (2026), Section 2.2.1, and Equations (18), (19), and (33) in Appendix B specify

\[ \frac{dH_{i,t}^{C}}{d\tau} =\widetilde n_{i,t}(\tau)E_{i,t}^{C}(k-\tau), \qquad 0\leq\tau\leq k \]

Substituting the surviving population from Equation (22) gives

\[ \widetilde n_{i,t}(\tau)=\widetilde n_{i,t}^{0} \exp\left[-(E_{i,t}^{\mathrm{sw}}+E_{i,t}^{\mathrm{fi}}) \left(k\tau-\frac{\tau^2}{2}\right)-\mu_i\tau\right] \]

The focal hunting count therefore depends on population depletion by both countries' hunting and non-hunting mortality

Mapping from paper notation to code

  • \( \tau \): tau, elapsed time since hunting starts
  • \( H_{i,t}^{C} \): H[1], cumulative expected focal-country hunting
  • \( \widetilde n_{i,t}^{0} \): n0, population at the start of the integration
  • \( k \): k, hunting duration and integration endpoint
  • \( E_{i,t}^{C} \): E_1, focal-country hunting pressure
  • The other country's hunting pressure: E_2
  • \( \mu_i \): mu, annual non-hunting mortality hazard

The derivative does not depend on H because the population is available analytically rather than included as another ODE state The caller integrates only from zero to k, outside which the paper sets the hunting derivative to zero The vector state and separate arguments implement Stan's current solver interface without changing the differential equation
