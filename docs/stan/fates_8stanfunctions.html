Equation (3) in Vanko et al (2026), Section 2.2.1, specifies the demographic transition as a multinomial over survival, hunting and other deaths

\[ \mathbf{u}_{a,s,t}^{1}\sim\mathrm{Multinomial}\left( n_{a,s,t},\left(\rho^{S},\rho^{H^{\mathrm{sw}}}, \rho^{H^{\mathrm{fi}}},\rho^{D}\right)_{a,s,t}\right) \]

Section 2.4 describes a continuous log-Gaussian approximation for fitting

This function uses a logistic-normal approximation rather than an exact multinomial draw

For the active categories, it constructs log-ratio means and a covariance factor from adjusted expected counts and returns

\[ \mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{active}}=N\operatorname{softmax} \left(\begin{bmatrix}0\\\mathbf{m}+L\mathbf{z}\end{bmatrix}\right) \]

This last expression documents the implementation, not a numbered paper equation

It gives continuous non-negative allocations summing to the supplied population

It does not imply exact multinomial moments

Mapping from paper notation to code

  • \( n_{a,s,t} \): demographic_population_count
  • \( n_{a,s,t}\rho^I_{a,s,t} \): the corresponding entry of expected_fate_counts
  • \( \mathbf{z} \): the required entries of fate_allocation_noise
  • \( \mathbf{m},L \): log-ratio mean and covariance factor computed below

Category order matches Equation (3): survival, Swedish hunting, Finnish hunting, other death or bycatch

Countries with zero quotas are omitted from the active categories and their allocations remain exactly zero, using one fewer innovation than active fates
